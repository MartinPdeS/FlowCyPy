Fluorescence from a labeled particle#

FlowCyPy predicts how much optical power a labeled particle delivers to each detector and when that power arrives. Three measurements in the output answer different questions. The event column Label:<dye name> gives molecules per particle. The event column named for the detector gives its prompt-equivalent power amplitude in watts. The same channel in record.signal.analog gives voltage versus time, including overlapping particles, lifetime, and electronics.

From molecule count to optical power#

For one particle, let \(I(t)\) be the local laser irradiance in W/m², \(N\) the number of molecules of one dye and \(\sigma_a\) the absorption cross section in m² per molecule at laser wavelength \(\lambda_L\). The source sets the peak irradiance at the event’s transverse position and generates the transit pulse from its arrival time and velocity. In the weak-excitation limit the absorbed photon rate is

\[\dot n_{\rm abs}(t) = \frac{N \sigma_a I(t)}{hc/\lambda_L}.\]

Quantum yield \(\Phi\) gives the mean number of emitted photons per absorbed photon. The dye’s normalized photon weights \(p_j\) sum to one over emission bins with representative wavelengths \(\lambda_j\). For detector \(d\), let \(f_d\) be the collected fraction of solid angle and \(T_d(\lambda_j)\) its filter transmission. Multiplying each bin’s photon rate by its photon energy gives the prompt-equivalent power

\[P^0_{dj}(t) = N \sigma_a I(t)\,\Phi\, f_d\,p_j\,T_d(\lambda_j) \frac{\lambda_L}{\lambda_j}.\]

The wavelength ratio matters when predicting voltage from a spectrum. At equal photon rates, the shorter-wavelength bin delivers more watts. For example, at an irradiance of \(10^6\) W/m², 20,000 molecules with a cross section of \(2\times10^{-20}\) m² absorb 400 pW. For excitation at 488 nm, yield 0.8, 20% geometric collection and all photons emitted and transmitted at 520 nm, the detector receives about 60 pW. Doubling the molecule count doubles this power. These values illustrate the calculation.

Choosing physical inputs#

  • Particle and dye. Sphere and core-shell populations support labels with explicit particle sampling. A dimensionless distribution supplies one nonnegative count per particle and dye, rounded to the nearest integer. Every detector sees that same draw. Multiple dyes add independently.

  • Absorption. Supply a molecular cross section at the configured laser wavelength. If a dye datasheet gives molar extinction coefficient in L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹, multiply its numerical value by 0.1 ln(10) and divide by the Avogadro constant to obtain m² per molecule (Beer–Lambert conversion). The model uses one local irradiance for every label on a particle. It treats cross section and quantum yield as constant through the transit. Saturation, bleaching, energy transfer, self-quenching and internal optical fields would require additional physics. The linear model can overpredict brightness at high excitation or high labeling density.

  • Spectrum. Supply integrated photon counts per bin as photon_weights. For measured photon density per nm, integrate over each bin width first. For radiant power per nm, also divide by the photon energy before integrating. Use a response-corrected spectrum if detector sensitivity shaped the measurement.

Surface and volume labeling#

To model a surface coating or dye distributed throughout a spherical bead, give a label its mean surface density or volume density. For each particle FlowCyPy draws a molecule count once, using the same sampled outer diameter \(D\) used to define that particle:

\[N \mid D \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\rho_A \pi D^2) \quad \text{or} \quad N \mid D \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\rho_V \pi D^3 / 6).\]
from FlowCyPy.dyes import fitc
from FlowCyPy.fluorescence import Label
from FlowCyPy.units import ureg

coating = Label(fluorophore=fitc(), molecules_per_area=0.02 / ureg.nm**2)

bulk_dye = Label(fluorophore=fitc(), molecules_per_volume=1_000 / ureg.micrometer**3)

Pass either label in the population’s labels argument. For a core-shell particle, volume loading includes both core and shell. The outer diameter is the core diameter plus twice the radial shell thickness. Even equal-sized beads fluctuate: at a fixed diameter, the count variance equals its mean. Size dispersion adds variation through the sampled area or volume. All channels use the stored event count, and an acquisition seed reproduces the draws. The model assumes independent occupancy and has no finite binding-site capacity. Use molecules_per_particle for a fixed count or a count distribution independent of geometry.

Predefined dyes#

Import a dye from FlowCyPy.dyes and call it to create a fluorophore. Set the labeling density separately for each population.

from FlowCyPy.dyes import alexa_fluor_488
from FlowCyPy.fluorescence import Label

label = Label(fluorophore=alexa_fluor_488(), molecules_per_particle=20_000)

Excitation. The default uses the peak extinction coefficient. For another laser line, pass relative_absorption equal to the ratio of absorbance at that line to absorbance at the maximum, taken from the same spectrum. Rebuild the dye with the appropriate ratio when changing lasers.

Emission. Each preset places all photons in one bin at its emission maximum. A hard filter therefore accepts either all or none of its emission. This approximation is useful for lifetime demonstrations. For filter throughput or spectral spillover, provide measured emission bins, for example with dataclasses.replace(dye, emission_wavelengths=wavelengths, photon_weights=counts). See Detect fluorescent beads alongside unlabeled beads for a complete acquisition.

Collection, filters and spillover#

Emission is isotropic. In the air collection geometry, \(0 \leq \mathrm{NA}_{\rm inner} \leq \mathrm{NA}_{\rm outer} \leq 1\). The central obscuration excludes the inner cone. Integrating over the remaining annulus gives the fraction of all emitted photons reaching the channel

\[f_d = \frac{\sqrt{1-\mathrm{NA}_{\rm inner}^2} -\sqrt{1-\mathrm{NA}_{\rm outer}^2}}{2}.\]

An unobscured NA of 0.8 collects 20%. Rotating that cone leaves its fluorescence fraction unchanged. Elastic scatter has its own angular dependence. Detectors default to scatter only. Select fluorescence only or both contributions using signals. A mixed channel applies its filter at the laser wavelength for scatter and at each emission wavelength for fluorescence.

A Bandpass(center=530 * ureg.nm, width=30 * ureg.nm) transmits unity from 515 through 545 nm inclusive and zero elsewhere. Omitting the filter accepts all wavelengths for the selected contributions. The entire weight of a bin is assigned to its representative wavelength. Split bins near a filter edge and repeat the calculation to check convergence. Real filter slopes, finite stopband leakage and other wavelength-dependent optical losses are outside this idealization. Spectral spillover still occurs whenever two dyes emit in the same passband. See Define custom dyes and predict spectral spillover.

Lifetime, transit and sampling#

Each dye can have its own lifetime \(\tau\). Zero or omitted lifetime gives prompt emission. For positive lifetime, the emitted power \(P_{dj}\) obeys

\[\tau \frac{dP_{dj}}{dt} + P_{dj} = P^0_{dj}(t).\]

This is a single exponential relaxation with unit integrated response. A finite lifetime shifts light into later bins. The area under a complete optical pulse is conserved, while the peak height and the energy inside a short trigger window can change. For an instantaneous excitation, the fraction collected in a gate of duration \(T\) starting at excitation is \(1-\exp(-T/\tau)\). A laser that stays on throughout the gate gives a different result because molecules continue to be excited inside the gate.

The digitizer sets the time grid, \(\Delta t=1/f_s\). FlowCyPy holds excitation constant within each bin and calculates the exact mean emitted power over that bin for the assumed excitation. Resolve the fastest change in the excitation and refine the grid to check your gate fraction or pulse shape. The solution starts in periodic steady state. A tail near the end of the acquisition continues at the start. To approximate an isolated pulse, leave enough dark time for its tail to decay before the next period.

The amplifier and analog circuits also shape the measured voltage. A faster digitizer can sample a bandwidth-limited waveform more densely, while its measured delay still includes the electronics. The worked example isolates the dye using a known excitation and idealized electronics. For an experimental comparison using scatter and fluorescence pulse delays, see Cao et al. (2014). See How much fluorescence arrives after the gate closes?.

Noise and comparison with measurements#

When enabled, laser relative intensity noise supplies a common excitation fluctuation across channels. Lifetime acts on this fluctuating excitation. Photon shot noise is then drawn using the energy of each wavelength component. For mean counts below 100 photons per bin the sampler uses a Poisson draw. At higher counts it uses a nonnegative Gaussian approximation with the same nominal mean and variance. Configured in-channel background uses the laser photon energy for this calculation.

Each detector sums optical powers and applies one responsivity in A/W across its entire band. Detector current noise and amplification follow. Run processing can then apply analog circuits, triggering, digitization and peak measurement. For an instrument comparison, match the laser irradiance, collection geometry, bandpass, responsivity, gain and bandwidth. Unlabeled controls establish the baseline and single-color controls reveal how much of each dye enters the other channels. Absolute intensity alone constrains a product of label count, absorption, yield and detection efficiency. Independent calibration is needed to infer molecule counts from voltage.
